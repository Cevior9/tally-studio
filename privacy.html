ClipOps

Privacy policy

Effective 27 October 2026

ClipOps is a private tool that Tally Studio uses to prepare short videos and publish them to its own YouTube channels. It is not offered to the public: only Tally Studio's owner signs in, and it connects only to channels Tally Studio controls.

YouTube API Services

ClipOps uses YouTube API Services. YouTube use is subject to the YouTube Terms of Service, and Google handles data under the Google Privacy Policy. ClipOps is not operated or endorsed by Google or YouTube.

What ClipOps receives from Google

When the owner connects a YouTube channel, Google asks the owner to approve access. The current connections use:

  • Upload videos (youtube.upload), to upload approved videos and schedule them.
  • View the YouTube account (youtube.readonly), to confirm the right channel is connected and read authorized video information when an implemented feature needs it.

If the owner configures a playlist, a new connection instead requests youtube.force-ssl. This is a wider permission covering video and playlist management, needed to add an uploaded video to that playlist. The owner reviews that permission before consent. Neither current connection uses it.

This version does not request yt-analytics.readonly, collect YouTube Analytics reports or send performance numbers to an AI provider. Declaring a scope in Google Cloud Console does not grant it to ClipOps.

ClipOps never receives the owner's Google password. It does not read viewer identities, comments or Gmail.

What ClipOps stores

  • The connected channel's ID and configured handle, granted permissions, project ID and connection timestamps/state.
  • Google access and refresh tokens, encrypted, plus short-lived connection state. Among runtime services, only the separate Publisher can read stored token ciphertext. The owner-facing API handles tokens during the connection and encrypts them for storage; AI agents cannot read the tokens.
  • When an upload is authorized: its YouTube video ID, link, metadata, schedule where applicable and upload status, plus quota and bounded error records. Resumable-upload session credentials are encrypted.

ClipOps also stores material the owner supplies: source videos, transcripts, rights evidence, titles and approval decisions. It never downloads videos from YouTube.

How the data is used and shared

YouTube connection data is used to verify the channel, authorize uploads and maintain the connection. Authorized video files, publication details and declarations go to Google through its official APIs. ClipOps runs on computers the operator controls. Connecting alone does not enable publication; normal publishing is currently off.

For separately authorized AI preparation and safety review, Anthropic processes independently supplied source transcripts, selected video frames, source/rights evidence, channel instructions and clip metadata. Google credentials, tokens and resumable-upload credentials are never sent to it. No YouTube Analytics reports are currently collected or sent to Anthropic.

ClipOps does not sell YouTube data or use it for advertising. Google processes authorized API requests, Anthropic processes the preparation inputs described above, and GitHub hosts these public information pages as described below.

Disconnecting and deleting data

  • The owner can choose Disconnect YouTube in ClipOps. The account is marked disconnected and uploads are blocked locally at once. The separate Publisher revocation service asks Google to revoke access, making at most five attempts with increasing delays.
  • When Google confirms revocation or says the token is already invalid, maintenance deletes the encrypted credentials and clears the canonical channel ID. If confirmation never arrives, credentials pending disconnection for at least seven days are deleted on a maintenance pass. These actions require the revocation service to remain running; local deletion does not prove Google revoked its grant.
  • Access can also be removed directly in Google Account connections. After doing that, disconnect locally as well.
  • Contact thanosgoik@gmail.com about deletion of other stored YouTube data. The current version does not automatically erase API-derived upload/history records or backups, and a complete verified erasure procedure and 30-day API-data refresh procedure are not implemented. Disconnect should not be understood as deleting all stored data.
  • Disconnecting does not delete videos already on YouTube. Those are managed in YouTube Studio.

Maintenance checks run every 30 seconds while the revocation service is online. Expired OAuth connection state, whose lifetime is nine minutes, is also removed by maintenance. Independently supplied media and rights evidence are separate local production records.

Security

Stored tokens are encrypted, database access is split by role, and private configuration is kept out of code repositories, logs and AI inputs. The trusted operator controls the computer, database and encryption-key backups.

The private ClipOps app uses an HttpOnly, SameSite=Strict owner-session cookie for sign-in and saves the light/dark preference in browser local storage. The current installation runs on localhost over HTTP; it does not claim to be an HTTPS-hosted public app. No advertising or usage-analytics tracker is included in the app.
